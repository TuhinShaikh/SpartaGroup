# NHS Test and Trace Programme

## Goals of the Programme
- Break chains of COVID-19 transmission by testing people and rapidly tracing and isolating their contacts, enabling a return to a "more normal way of life."
- Act as an alternative to national lockdowns — then-health secretary Matt Hancock said it would let the government replace lockdowns with "individual isolation" for contacts of positive cases.

## Why It Was Considered a Failure
- A Public Accounts Committee (PAC) report found it had not achieved its main objective of helping to break chains of COVID-19 transmission and enable a return to normal life, despite receiving £37 billion over two years — nearly 20% of the entire 2020–21 NHS England budget.
- Performance was patchy and wasteful: only 14% of 691 million lateral flow tests sent out had results reported, and less than half of the contact tracing staff hired were ever in use at any one time, with just 11% of contact tracers working during the February lockdown.

## Who Was Running the Programme
- It was originally run by Baroness Dido Harding, who stepped back from the role in April 2021, before the programme moved into the newly created UK Health Security Agency (UKHSA).
- Day-to-day contact tracing relied heavily on outsourced call centres, including outsourcing firm Serco, alongside a large number of expensive external consultants.

## Impact of It Going Wrong
- Financially, it became one of the most costly pandemic programmes ever run, with excessive reliance on expensive consultants and, per the PAC, continued over-reliance on consultants likely to cost taxpayers hundreds of millions of pounds going forward.
- Its central promise of averting further lockdowns was not met — since the programme started running, England entered two nationwide lockdowns, undermining public confidence that the huge spend had delivered value or protection.
